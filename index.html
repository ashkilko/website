Biography

About my work

My research examines securities trading and the structure of financial markets. At its core, it seeks to understand whether securities markets operate efficiently and in ways that serve the broader interests of society. My work has appeared in leading academic journals, including the Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, Management Science, and Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, among others.

Most recently, my focus has been on retail execution quality in the United States and Canada; market innovation, whereby markets introduce new technologies and new trading sessions; continuous versus periodic auction market design; the conduits of price discovery and how information disseminates across markets; how modern algorithms trade on this information; and how insiders participate in markets. Much of this work bears on market regulation, and several of my studies evaluate specific market rules and practices in the United States, Canada, and around the globe.

My work has received a number of awards, including the Journal of Finance Dimensional Fund Advisors First Prize. Other awards have come from academic associations such as the Eastern Finance Association (twice), the Financial Management Association, the Midwest Finance Association, the Northern Finance Association, the Spanish Finance Association, and the Swiss Society for Financial Market Research; from conferences including the Auckland Finance Meeting, the Mid-Atlantic Research Conference in Finance, and the Market Regulation Services-DeGroote Conference; and from the Toronto CFA Society (twice).

Over the years, I have received research grants from numerous organizations, including the Canadian Derivatives Institute, Canada Foundation for Innovation, Canada Research Chairs program, Jan Wallander and Tom Hedelius Foundation (Sweden), Nasdaq Educational Foundation (United States), Ontario Ministry of Research and Innovation, Ontario Research Fund, Ontario Securities Commission, and the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada.

Beyond the university setting, I have worked with financial regulators in Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States, as well as with various industry organizations and market participants. This work has ranged from designing a trading fee pilot study for the Canadian Securities Administrators to reviewing major policy documents, and it keeps my research connected to the questions that regulators and market participants face in practice.

In parallel with my own research, I serve as an organizer of The Microstructure Exchange, a global online seminar series that features leading researchers presenting new work on market structure. The series aims to promote collaboration and knowledge exchange among academics, industry practitioners, and regulators worldwide. I also serve as Associate Program Chair of the Northern Finance Association, on the program committees of major finance conferences, and as a grant reviewer for national research funding agencies around the world.
